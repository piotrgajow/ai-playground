# Human – Cleric (War Domain)

*Zealous, grim, battle-hungry* · Level 5 · D&D 5e (2014)

> A navy chaplain who decided the Foehammer loves the battle, not the flag, and now blesses the guns of pirates.

## Background

The character spent a decade as chaplain on the *Resolute*, a navy frigate, leading the crew's prayers to Tempus, Lord of Battles. They took the sermons seriously: Tempus favors no nation and no side. He favors the fight itself and whoever fights it bravely. When a pirate fleet cornered the frigate, the admiral surrendered without firing a shot. To the character, surrendering to cowardice was the one real sin. They killed the admiral during the handover, and the pirates, impressed, invited them aboard. They have sailed under the black flag ever since, fighting for crews that at least *fight*. **Hook/secret for the GM:** the character still wears the admiral's signet ring on a cord under their armor. The navy has a quiet bounty out for "the chaplain who murdered an admiral," and at least one old crewmate from the *Resolute* is still out there somewhere and remembers their face.

## Playstyle

The character is the party's anchor: the plated figure standing in the middle of a boarding party. They open a fight with **Spirit Guardians**, walk into the densest crowd and let the aura grind enemies down. They add **Spiritual Weapon** (a spectral boarding axe) and use their bonus action to swing it each turn, and hit with a real weapon on top of that. Against a boss they save **War Priest** for extra attacks and use **Guided Strike** to guarantee the blow that matters. When the rogue gets in over their head, a **Healing Word** from across the deck keeps them standing. Outside combat they lead the crew's prayers, use **Thaumaturgy** to make their voice boom over the waves like thunder, and read enemy captains with Insight.

## Vibe

A soldier-priest with no warmth and no hypocrisy. They don't pretend pirating is noble; they believe battle is holy and courage is the only virtue that counts. They pray quietly before every fight and speak the names of worthy foes afterward so the Foehammer remembers them. They respect brave enemies, even ones they've just killed, and have open contempt for cowards, including cowardly allies. Their humor is dry and rare.

- *"Tempus doesn't care whose flag you fly. He cares whether you fought for it."*
- *"Surrender? I've seen what surrender looks like. I cut its throat."*
- *"He fought well. Remember his face. The Foehammer will."*

## Visual description

Broad-shouldered, in their late thirties or early forties, with a shaved head and a salt-and-pepper beard cut short to fit under a helmet. They still wear their old navy chain mail, blackened with tar to keep it from rusting. The navy insignia is ripped off, and a silver sword on a blood-red field, Tempus's symbol, is painted in its place. They carry a battered round shield with the same flaming sword hammered into it and a heavy boarding axe on the hip. A string of iron beads hangs at the belt, one for each worthy foe they've killed. Several bullet and blade scars mark the forearms. The signet ring is hidden under the collar.

## Personality

- **Traits:** calm under pressure, blunt to the point of cruelty, disciplined in a crew that has no discipline.
- **Ideal:** battle is sacred and courage is the only virtue. Victory without bravery is empty.
- **Bond:** Tempus and the crew that fights beside them, but only as long as that crew keeps its nerve.
- **Flaw:** they can't stand retreat or surrender, even when it's the smart move, and will push a losing fight rather than back down.
- **With the party:**
  - They respect the **swashbuckler**'s nerve but find their flamboyance vain.
  - They're uneasy but pragmatic about the **necromancer**: raising the dead is an insult to a warrior's death, but undead that win battles are hard to argue with.
  - They treat the **bard** as a useful herald who spreads word of the crew's victories.

## Build notes

- **Race:** Variant Human, with +1 Strength and +1 Wisdom, a level-1 feat and one extra skill.
- **Stats:** point buy STR 15, DEX 8, CON 14, INT 8, WIS 15, CHA 10, which becomes **STR 16, WIS 16, CON 14** after racial bonuses. At level 4 take +2 Wisdom, for **WIS 18**: spell save DC 15 and 4 uses of War Priest.
- **Feat (level 1): War Caster.**
  - It gives advantage on Constitution saves to keep concentration, which matters for Spirit Guardians and Crusader's Mantle.
  - It also lets you cast spells with a weapon and a shield in hand.
  - Skip Polearm Master: your bonus action is already taken by War Priest, Spiritual Weapon and Healing Word.
- **Armor and weapons:** chain mail (AC 16) plus a shield gives **AC 18**. Use a battleaxe or warhammer (d8, d10 two-handed) with the shield.
- **Skills:**
  - Background (Sailor): Athletics, Perception.
  - Cleric: Insight, Religion.
  - Variant Human: Intimidation.
- **Cantrips (4):** Toll the Dead, Word of Radiance (damages everyone adjacent), Guidance, Thaumaturgy.
- **Spells:**
  - **Always prepared (domain):** Divine Favor, Shield of Faith, Magic Weapon, Spiritual Weapon, Crusader's Mantle, Spirit Guardians.
  - **Prepared (9):** Healing Word, Bless, Command, Guiding Bolt, Aid, Hold Person, Lesser Restoration, Revivify, Dispel Magic.
- **Main combo:** Spirit Guardians on turn 1, then Spiritual Weapon on turn 2 while keeping a melee attack going.
- **Crusader's Mantle:** use it instead of Spirit Guardians when the rogue and any undead are all attacking, since it adds +1d4 radiant to every ally hit within 30 ft.
- **Channel Divinity:** 1 per short rest. Use Guided Strike on a critical hit attempt (a big attack that has to land), or use it on Harness Divine Power to regain a 2nd-level slot.
- **Traps to avoid:**
  - War Priest uses come back only on a long rest, so don't waste them on weak enemies.
  - All of your buffs and Spirit Guardians need concentration, and you can hold only one at a time. Don't cast Shield of Faith over Spirit Guardians.
  - Heavy armor is bad at sea: disadvantage on Stealth, and the GM may make swimming painful. Ask the GM how they rule falling overboard in armor.
